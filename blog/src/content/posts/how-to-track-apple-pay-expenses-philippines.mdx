Apple Pay is finally live in the Philippines. Since **August 4, 2026**, Filipino iPhone users can add their cards to Apple Wallet and tap to pay in stores, in apps, and on the web — no more reaching for a physical card at Jollibee or 7-Eleven.

Which also means a very familiar problem just arrived: contactless payments are so effortless that by Friday you have no idea where the week's money went. This guide covers what works at launch — supported banks, merchants, apps — and then the part nobody else is writing about: how to make **every Apple Pay tap log itself automatically** into [Expensa](https://apps.apple.com/app/expensa-ai-expenses-manager/id6758392205), a privacy-first expense tracker, with a one-time 2-minute setup.

## Which banks support Apple Pay in the Philippines?

At launch, four banks let you add debit and credit cards to Apple Wallet:

- **UnionBank**
- **GoTyme Bank**
- **Metrobank**
- **Chinabank**

**BPI** has completed card tokenization and is expected to activate support shortly after launch. **BDO**, Security Bank, and other major banks haven't announced dates yet — more issuers typically join in the months after an Apple Pay launch, as happened in every other market.

Adding a card takes a minute: open **Wallet → "+" → Debit or Credit Card**, scan or enter your card, and verify via SMS or a phone call from your bank.

## Where does Apple Pay work in the Philippines?

Anywhere with a contactless terminal — which after years of QR Ph and tap-enabled cards is most of Metro Manila retail. Confirmed working at launch:

- **In stores:** Jollibee, Starbucks, 7-Eleven, Power Mac Center, ZARA, Abenson, Ayala Malls
- **In apps and online:** foodpanda, Shopee, Rustan's — any checkout that shows the Apple Pay button

You pay by double-clicking the side button and holding your iPhone or Apple Watch near the terminal. The card number is never shared with the merchant — Apple Pay uses a device-specific token.

## The problem: taps are easy, tracking is not

Every tap is one less receipt, one less cash count — and one more expense you'll forget by tomorrow. The usual fixes all have catches: bank apps show transactions per-card (good luck if you tap with three cards), statements arrive monthly, and aggregators like Plaid don't cover Philippine banks at all.

The good news: iOS has a built-in way to fix this, and it works with every launch bank.

- **No bank connection needed.** The automation runs locally on your iPhone using an official iOS trigger — your bank login never leaves your head.
- **It's real-time.** The transaction lands in Expensa the second the terminal beeps, not at the end of the month.
- **It works with any card in Wallet.** UnionBank, GoTyme, Metrobank, Chinabank today — and any bank that joins later, automatically.
- **PHP is fully supported.** Set the Philippine peso as your base currency; foreign-currency taps convert at live exchange rates.

## What you'll need

- iPhone with Apple Pay set up
- [Expensa](https://apps.apple.com/app/expensa-ai-expenses-manager/id6758392205) installed
- Shortcuts app (built into iOS — no download needed)

## Step 1: Open the Shortcuts app

Find the **Shortcuts** app on your iPhone and open it. It comes pre-installed on every iPhone running iOS 13 or later — if you've never opened it, it's probably in the Utilities folder.

![Shortcuts app on iPhone home screen](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-1-shortcuts-app.png)

## Step 2: Go to the Automation tab and tap "New Automation"

At the bottom of the screen, tap the **Automation** tab. Then tap the **New Automation** button.

![Automation tab in Shortcuts](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-2-automation-tab.png)

## Step 3: Select "Wallet"

Scroll down to find **Wallet** — specifically the trigger **"When I tap a Wallet Card or Pass"**. This fires the automation every time you use Apple Pay.

![Selecting the Wallet Transaction trigger](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-3-wallet-trigger.png)

## Step 4: Select your cards and set to Run Immediately

Pick the cards you want tracked — your UnionBank debit, Metrobank credit card, GoTyme card, all of them. Then change the run mode to **Run Immediately**. This is important: if it's set to "Run After Confirmation", Shortcuts will ask for your approval every single time you pay, which defeats the whole point.

![Disabling Ask Before Running](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-4-ask-before-running.png)

## Step 5: Tap "Create New Shortcut"

When prompted to choose what the automation does, tap **Create New Shortcut**. This opens the action editor where you'll add the Expensa action.

![New Blank Automation screen](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-5-new-blank-automation.png)

## Step 6: Search for and add the Expensa action

In the search bar, type **Expensa** or **Tap to Pay Automation** and select it from the results. This is the Expensa action that receives the transaction data.

![Searching for the Expensa action](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-6-search-action.png)

## Step 7: Map the transaction fields

Connect the Apple Pay data to the Expensa fields. For each field in the action, tap it → select **Variable** → then **Shortcut Input**, and choose the matching field:

- **Transaction Amount** → Amount
- **Merchant Name** → Merchant Name
- **Transaction Date** → Date *(optional)*

![Mapping transaction fields to variables](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-7-map-fields.png)

![Selecting Shortcut Input for the variable](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-7-1-map-fields.png)

## Step 8: Tap "Done" to save

Tap **Done** in the top-right corner. Your automation is live.

From this point on, every Apple Pay tap — the morning coffee, the foodpanda order, the groceries at the mall — silently logs itself to Expensa with merchant, amount, and date. No action needed from you.

![Completed automation](/blog/images/apple-pay-setup/step-8-done.png)

## Set the peso as your base currency

In Expensa, go to **Settings → Default Currency** and pick **PHP (₱)**. Every report, budget, and chart will show pesos. If you pay in another currency — a USD subscription, shopping abroad, an OFW managing money across two countries — Expensa stores the original amount and converts it to PHP at the live rate, so your totals always make sense.

## Let categories sort themselves

Expensa recognizes merchants over time. Assign "Jollibee" to *Food & Drinks* once and every future tap there is categorized automatically; on the premium plan, AI categorizes new merchants for you the moment the transaction arrives. You can also get a notification if a tap pushes a category over its budget — before the next tap, not at the end of the month.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Apple Pay available in the Philippines?**
Yes. Apple Pay officially launched in the Philippines on August 4, 2026. You can add debit and credit cards from UnionBank, GoTyme Bank, Metrobank, and Chinabank to Apple Wallet and pay with iPhone or Apple Watch anywhere contactless payments are accepted.

**Does BDO or BPI support Apple Pay in the Philippines?**
Not at launch. BPI has completed card tokenization and is expected to activate Apple Pay support shortly. BDO and other banks have not announced a date yet. Until your bank joins, you can still track those card expenses in Expensa manually or by importing your bank's CSV statement.

**Does Apple Pay work with GCash or Maya?**
No. GCash, Maya, and QR Ph payments don't go through Apple Pay, so they can't be added to Apple Wallet and won't trigger the Shortcuts automation. Log those expenses manually or import them via CSV.

**Does the automation capture Apple Pay purchases in apps like Shopee or foodpanda?**
Yes — as long as you check out with Apple Pay. The Wallet trigger fires for in-person taps and Apple Pay purchases in apps and on the web. If you type your card number manually at checkout, it won't fire.

**Do I need to keep Expensa open for auto-tracking to work?**
No. The Shortcuts automation runs silently in the background and Expensa confirms each imported transaction with a local notification, even when the app is closed.
